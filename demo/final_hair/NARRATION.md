# Narration: 1:45 demo, Track 1

The script is about 245 words, at roughly 140 words per minute. Each block fits its scene; the word
counts are in brackets. Every number spoken here appears on screen and comes from
`validation_results.txt` or `demo_assets/`.

**Scene 1, 0:00–0:10 [27]**
XR gaze estimators need huge, exactly labelled eye datasets. Real capture is slow, costly and
restricted by privacy. We built a synthetic alternative in Blender.

**Scene 2, 0:10–0:25 [40]**
This is our actual scene: a photoreal scanned head from 3D Scan Store, rigged with left and right eye bones. Each eye rotates
about its own centre. The fixation target sits thirty-five centimetres ahead of the eye midpoint, and
the render camera is locked at forty.

**Scene 3, 0:25–0:55 [72]**
Everything is driven by our Python wrapper, run here on the extracted release package. eyes.reset
looks at the centre. move minus one, zero: both pupils shift left in the image. Move one, zero: right.
Zero, one: up. Zero, minus one: down. And reset returns exactly to centre. Each call to eyes.render
returns the input, both eye rotations, and the pixel position of each pupil, shown as the yellow cross.

**Scene 4, 0:55–1:15 [45]**
The required sequence: ten frames, starting at the top and moving clockwise. Frame k uses theta equals
two pi k over ten, with h equal to sine theta and v equal to cosine theta. Both eyes converge on the
moving target every time.

**Scene 5, 1:15–1:30 [36]**
Every render has a metadata record: the frame number, theta, the h and v inputs, world-frame rotations
for both eyes, the two-D pupil centres as seen through the cornea, and the camera matrix.

**Scene 6, 1:30–1:45 [36]**
Results: the pupil ground truth matches a lighting-free mask to within a fraction of a pixel, all API tests
pass, ten valid renders, and metadata regenerated byte for byte. A deterministic, calibrated, reproducible pipeline for synthetic
gaze data.
